The Rise of the 15-Minute City

Imagine a city where everything you need—your home, work, school, groceries, parks—is just a short walk or bike ride away. That’s the promise of the 15-minute city, a revolutionary concept in urban planning aimed at decentralizing urban life.

By designing neighborhoods to be self-sufficient, this model reduces dependence on cars, cuts carbon emissions, and promotes healthier, more engaged communities. Cities like Paris, Melbourne, and Portland are pioneering this shift.

“The 15-minute city empowers people to live locally and reconnect with their communities.” — Carlos Moreno

Critics argue that such transformations are idealistic, especially in cities shaped by decades of car-centric infrastructure. Yet, small steps—like local zoning reform, bike lane expansion, and community-led design—are already making a difference.
