Conclusion

Russi'a mortality crisis has been driven by a deeply mebedded drinking culture, with male life expectancy dropping to 57.6 years in 1994 and up to 33% of all deaths linked to alcohol. Binge drinking of spritis like vodka, especially among working-age men, contributed to high rates of poisoning, cardiovascular failure, and traume, creating on of the world's largest gender gaps in life expectancy.

From the mid-2000s, goverment reforms such as EGAIS, excise tax hikes, and price controls helped reduce alcohol sales, particularly vodka, and shifted prefrences toward lower-alcohol beverages. These measures correlated with declining mortality, though regional differences persisted due to variations in enforcement and cultural norms. While national policies reshaped consumption and improved health outcomes, the enduring gender and regional disparities reveal the ongoing challenges in addressing alcohol-related harm in Russia.
