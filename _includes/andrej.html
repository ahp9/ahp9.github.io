In the introduction (Figure 1), we saw that Russia leads in alcohol-related deaths. According to official Russian statistics, alcohol-related deaths among working-age males accounted for around 4-5% of all deaths during the peak in the 1990s. This was approximately five times lower than the leading cause of death—diseases of the circulatory system—which accounted for about 23%. It's important to note that alcohol-related deaths here refer only to those directly attributed to alcohol consumption under the ICD-10 classification. This excludes many indirectly related fatalities, such as traffic accidents. As a result, the actual number is higher; a case study in the early 2000s estimated that hazardous drinking contributed to 40% of all deaths in one Russian city (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17574092/).

Figure x: Percentage of total deaths by alcohol-related and circulatory system disease causes in Russia. Alcohol-related causes include: Chronic alcoholism, alcoholic psychosis, encephalopathy, dementia, Alcoholic liver disease and Unintentional poisoning by alcohol (ICD-10 codes: F10, K70, X45). Diseases of the circulatory system include: Rheumatic heart disease, Hypertensive disease, Ischaemic heart disease, Cerebrovascular disease (ICD-10 codes: I00-I99).

The correlation is particularly evident during three periods: the sharp mortality increase following the Soviet collapse (1991-1994), the and the sustained decline after the implementation of alcohol control policies (post-2006).
